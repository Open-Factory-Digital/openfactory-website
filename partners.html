The program
← Back to openfactory.digital Certification and partners

The code is free. The name is earned.

Anyone may run, fork and sell OpenFactory. What only the project grants is the right to say that a person, a company, a build or a hosted service is OpenFactory-certified, and that grant is worth exactly the process behind it. This page is the short form; every rule links to the document that holds it.

The program documents live in docs/program of the core repository. This page reflects version 1.0 of the program.

Method

Seven phases, each with a gate you can read

A certified implementation follows the delivery method: the platform's own onboarding path with the parts around it a client organisation needs. Every phase ends on a command's output, not a slide.

PhaseWhat is decidedEvidence
0 Qualifywhether to assess at all: a runnable test command, a route to the agent, a supported forge, someone who will read the pull requestsa one-page note
1 Assesswhat the factory can do on this codebase, what it costs, what must change firstenv read, env context, a scorecard, a report
2 Designevery decision the deployment embodies: shape, providers, credentials, the box, the floor, merge policy, what follows a mergethe design record, signed
3 Implementinstall, onboard, prove, rehearse, first ticket, a costed pilot, hand-overdoctor, box status, env rehearse, the pilot report
4 Operatethe cadences: attention, parked cards, cost against budget, proofsthe operator's log, the cost dashboard
5 Supportseverities from the platform's own failure classes; response times by tier; escalation to the maintainersthe ticket record
6 Maintainupgrades, proofs, rotation, retention, backup and a tested restorethe maintenance calendar

Three profiles, one platform

Light is one team on one machine or one small stack. Standard is a hosted forge, a real board and several people on one panel. Enterprise adds a signed security review, identity, a promotion chain with a human production gate, and audit.

A profile is not an edition. Every profile runs the same Apache-2.0 build with every feature. A profile is the set of controls a certified implementation must have in place, written so an auditor can check each one.

What the method never does

  • Merge a proposal into a client's repository. The client merges; the platform proposes.
  • Spend before proving. The box is proven, the rehearsal is costed, the pilot has a cap.
  • Sell what the status page says is not built.
  • Restate the platform's behaviour. The documentation is the syllabus.

People

Seven credentials, one per role

A certification is held by a person, never by a company. It expires. It is verifiable by number. The roles that touch a deployment are examined by doing the work in a lab built on the one-machine door, with a stub agent, so an examination costs no tokens and grades the same for everyone.

Associate

For everyone on an engagement: requesters, reviewers, sponsors. Multiple choice, 75 minutes, three years. No prerequisite.

Operator

Runs a deployment: installs, keeps doctor green, answers what parks, upgrades, rotates, backs up, restores. Performance-based, two hours, two years.

Implementer

Takes a codebase from assessment to go-live. Performance-based, three hours, two years. Requires a valid Operator credential.

Solution Architect

Designs a deployment and signs its security review. A design record graded to a rubric plus a scenario examination. Requires a valid Implementer credential.

Product Owner

Owns what gets built: requirements, the baseline, the confirmations, the release. Two hours, three years. No prerequisite.

Developer

Writes an add-on the core does not ship, to the extension model and the house rules, and proves it with the conformance suite. Three hours, two years.

Trainer

Teaches the program's courses. Holds Implementer or Solution Architect, passes a teach-back under observation, renewed yearly on delivery records.

The blueprints, the examination rules, the courses and the proposed fees are in docs/program/03-role-certifications.md. A credential says a person performed the role's tasks to the program's standard on a given day. It claims nothing more, and no partner may imply that it does.

Companies

Three tiers, counted in people and deployments

A partner is counted the way every program in the market counts one: the certified people it employs, the deployments it can show, and what it gives back. The rules apply identically to every company, the project's own affiliates included.

registered

Any company that has accepted the code of conduct. Listed by name. May say "Powered by OpenFactory".

certified

Three certified people, at least one Implementer and one Operator; one referenceable deployment with the method's evidence; a support tier from the standard.

premier

Six certified people including a Solution Architect; three referenceable deployments, one Enterprise; an audited support desk; a maintained upstream contribution.

Specialisations

Implementation, which every certified partner holds. Managed Hosting, only for a service that passes the hosting conformance. Support, for an audited desk. Training, for a partner with a certified trainer and the courseware licence. Technology, for an add-on listed as OpenFactory Compatible.

What a partner owes

  • The method, on every implementation it calls certified, with the evidence producible.
  • Honesty about what the platform does not do, before the design record is signed.
  • Upstream first: defects reported, fixes proposed, no divergent fork under the name.
  • A named roster, kept current, never shared between partners.

The tiers, the benefits, the obligations, the verification and the revocation rules are in docs/program/04-partner-program.md. The partner directory is published on this page once the first partners are listed.

The name

What may be called OpenFactory

Apache-2.0 grants every right to the code and none to the mark. A build, a hosted service or an add-on earns the name by passing a test anyone can run, submitted in public, valid for a version, refreshed yearly.

01

Certified OpenFactory, for a build

The public test suite green in both orders on the build, every shipped provider row passing conformance-adapter, and the reference project through doctor, conformance, box prove and a rehearsal. A build may add behaviour; it may not remove the floor, the credential boundary, the pipeline's control of version control, or the human production gate.

02

OpenFactory Certified Hosting, for a service

A certified build run to the hosting standard: one deployment per client organisation, the credential boundary kept between provider and client, the data's location declared, the backup set exportable so a client can leave, the panel behind identity, version currency, a tested restore, support at Premium or above.

03

OpenFactory Compatible, for an add-on

Each declared row passing conformance-adapter on the current and previous minor, with a support commitment on the listing. The core's documentation never names an add-on as more than an add-on; the compatible list is where a deployment looks.

The submission, one pull request 
v0.4/<vendor>-<product>/
PRODUCT.yaml  README.md  suite-fixed.xml  suite-random.xml
adapters.txt  preflight.json  doctor.txt  conformance.txt
box-prove.txt  rehearse.txt

A bot checks the files within minutes; a reviewer checks the participation form and the reproduction within five business days; the merge is the certification. Free for open-source distributions and non-profits. Anyone may say, truthfully, that a product runs or is built on OpenFactory; nobody may put the name in their own product's name.

Funding

How the project pays for this

What stays free, forever

  • The platform, with every feature. There is no enterprise edition.
  • The documentation, including the program's.
  • The conformance suite and the right to run it.
  • Descriptive use of the name, and listing for open-source distributions.

What the project earns from

Examinations, partner tiers, commercial conformance listings, the courseware licence, and membership in what the project becomes. The project never sells implementation, hosting or support to a client, and takes no share of a partner's revenue.

The marks and the program move to a neutral holder when a second partner of scale exists, and to a foundation with members when three independent companies want a seat. Until then the steward runs the program by these documents, and the conflict-of-interest rules apply from the first day.

docs/program/07-sustainability.md carries the revenue lines, the proposed fee bands, the foundation path and the governance.
